## Problem

A caretaker who connects Google Calendar trusts it as a mirror of Caretook. Today it can damage the record: tidying old events out of the Google calendar cancels past events in Caretook and marks todos done for the whole group. It can also lose things: an event made in Google more than 90 days ahead never shows up in Caretook, and nothing ever looks at it again. A provider deleted in Caretook is still named in Google events.

Reminder emails have their own faults. One can arrive after its event if mail was failing for a while. It gives a time with no zone and in a 24-hour style, so a family member in another city can't tell when the visit is. And a member who agreed to share details under one wording is never asked again when the wording changes, although the version is stored for exactly that.

Last, a repeat set "monthly on the 31st" silently skips every shorter month, and there is no way to say "the last day of the month".

## Solution

- **Google can't rewrite the past or finish work.** Deleting a Google event for something that has already started changes nothing in Caretook. Deleting an upcoming todo's event never marks it done; the event comes back.
- **Far-off events arrive.** An event made in Google up to a year ahead appears in Caretook within minutes, and Caretook events up to a year ahead appear in Google. Anything further, and each instance of a Google repeat, arrives as it comes into range, through a daily catch-up.
- **Deleted providers aren't named** in Google events.
- **A reminder never arrives late.** It expires when its event starts.
- **A changed consent wording asks again** on the Details card. Until the member agrees to the current wording, everything leaving Caretook for them is generic.
- **Times say whose clock.** "Thu, Nov 19 · 9:00–10:00 AM PST".
- **"On the last day"** is a monthly repeat choice, and the choice that skips short months says so.

## User stories

1. As a caretaker, I want deleting an old event in my Google calendar to leave the Caretook event as it was, so that tidying my calendar never rewrites the care record. (B-304)
2. As a group member, I want a todo to stay open when someone deletes its event in Google, so that work isn't marked done by accident. (B-304)
3. As a caretaker, I want an upcoming todo I deleted in Google to come back there, so that Google keeps showing what is still to do. (B-304)
4. As a caretaker, I want an event I make in Google for four months from now to show in Caretook within minutes, so that the group sees a visit as soon as it is booked. (B-305)
5. As a caretaker, I want a Caretook event four months out to show in my Google calendar, so that I see it where I plan. (B-305)
6. As a caretaker, I want an event beyond a year, or a later instance of a Google repeat, to arrive within a day of coming into range, so that nothing made in Google is lost for good. (B-305)
7. As a member, I want a reminder email that could not be sent before its event began to be dropped, so that I never get "tomorrow at 9:00" after the visit. (B-306)
8. As a caretaker, I want a provider I deleted to stop appearing in Google events, so that removed names don't linger outside Caretook. (B-307)
9. As a member, I want the Details card to tell me when the wording I agreed to has changed and let me agree again, so that my consent matches what is shared. (B-308)
10. As a member whose consent is out of date, I want my feed, Google events and reminders to go generic until I agree again, so that nothing is shared under wording I never saw. (B-308)
11. As a family member in another time zone, I want a reminder's time to name its zone and use AM and PM, so that I know when the visit is. (B-309)
12. As a writer, I want to repeat an event or a todo on the last day of every month, so that month-end tasks land in every month. (B-309)
13. As a writer who picks the 29th, 30th or 31st, I want to be told that shorter months are skipped, so that the gap is not a surprise. (B-309)

## Implementation decisions

**The pull planner** (`interpretGoogleChange`, pure). Its interface stays one function returning one action per change.

- A delete of a started item (an event or a todo; the planner's existing "started" rule, an all-day item from the start of its day) is `ignore` with a new reason, `started`. A started visit of a repeat keeps `overwrite: pastVisit` for time changes, as today.
- A delete of an upcoming todo's event is `overwrite` (a new reason, `todoDelete`). The `completeTodo` action is removed from the planner and the service.
- A new Google event is taken when it starts inside the Google horizon: 90 days back to 365 ahead, or 90 ahead when the event is an instance of a Google repeat. `GoogleEvent` gains `recurring: boolean` (from Google's recurring event id), set by the real and the fake client.
- A linked item is looked up in the Google horizon, so a time change in Google to an event eight months out is applied, not ignored as gone.

**The Google horizon.** One client-safe rule beside `calendarWindow`: `googleWindow(now)` (90 back, 365 ahead). The sync service's group read (its only callers are push and pull) takes it; the feed and the reminder sweep have their own reads and keep `calendarWindow`. The push planner is unchanged: it diffs whatever items it is given, so a far event is written once and deleted only when it leaves the horizon behind or is deleted.

**The daily catch-up.** The Google client gains `listWindow(calendarId, from, to)`: live events only (no deleted ones), repeats expanded, bounded by the horizon, paged like the change list. `google_calendar_connection` gains a nullable `last_catch_up_at` (adds a migration). A pull whose connection has no catch-up in the last 24 hours also lists the window and runs the same planner over the events that have **no link**; linked events are skipped (the change list owns them). The time is recorded only when the catch-up finishes, and the sync token is neither read nor written by it. A failed catch-up doesn't fail the pull; it runs again at the next one.

**Provider names.** The sync service's provider read joins live providers only, in the same group, as the reminder read does. The feed already reads live providers through the model; a test now says so.

**Consent.** One pure rule in the calendar rules: `hasDetailsConsent({ consentAt, consentVersion })` is true only when both are set and the version equals `DETAILS_CONSENT_VERSION`. Every reader uses it: the settings view, the feed's resolve, the push's member read and the reminder inputs. The settings view gains `detailsConsentStale` (agreed, but to an older version). Turning the switch on while stale records a fresh time and the current version (today it is a no-op when a time exists). No migration and no write to existing rows; a bump takes effect at the next read, and the daily push rewrites each stale member's Google events as generic within a day.

**The Details card.** When the member's consent is out of date: a line under the switch says the wording changed since they agreed on the stored day and that turning it on agrees again. The switch shows off. The device's cached copy carries the flag so the card reads the same offline.

**Reminder expiry.** `notification_outbox` gains a nullable `expires_at` (adds a migration). `notify` takes an optional `expiresAt`. Delivery: a claimed row whose expiry has passed is marked `skipped` with error `expired`, without calling the sender, and is treated from there like any other skipped row (redaction, purge); a failed send whose next attempt would fall after the expiry is marked the same way at once. The staff mail view names the reason where it names the others. The reminder planner's sends gain `expiresAt` from its existing "too late" rule, and the sweep passes it. Other mail has no expiry and behaves as now. A delivery run's summary counts expired rows.

**Time wording** (`calendarWhen`, `calendarText`). US English day and 12-hour time, a shared AM/PM written once when both ends match ("9:00–10:00 AM", "11:30 AM–1:00 PM"), and the zone's short name after a timed range, from `Intl` for the instant shown (so daylight time reads PDT). All-day text has no zone. The interface is unchanged: every caller gets the new text. Every Google event's hash changes once, so the first push after landing rewrites each connected member's events; that is expected.

**The repeat rule.** `MonthlyBy` gains `{ by: "lastDay" }`, written as `BYMONTHDAY=-1`; the parser accepts it (no other negative day), the expander passes it to `rrule`, and the rule's wording says "on the last day". A repeating todo's next day comes from the same expander, so it follows with no change of its own. The Repeat sheet's monthly choices come from the pure choice module: "On the last day" is added when the first day is the last day of its month, and the by-date label for the 29th to 31st carries "skips shorter months". A stored last-day rule keeps its choice when edited from another day, as a kept ordinal weekday does today.

**Two migrations** (the outbox column, the connection column), in different waves. **No new dependency, route, table, permission or sync stream.**

## Testing decisions

A good test here states what a member sees or what leaves Caretook, not how a function is arranged.

- **Unit:** the pull planner (a table of change, link, item, role and time to action: deletes of started and upcoming events and todos, repeats, far and recurring new events, linked far items); `googleWindow`; the consent rule; `calendarWhen` and `calendarText` (same and mixed AM/PM, across midnight, all-day, a daylight-time date) and the golden `.ics` files; the reminder planner's `expiresAt`; the repeat rule (text, parse, wording), the expander and the todo's next day for the last day across February and a leap year; the monthly choices. Prior art: `pullPlan.test.ts`, the `calendarText` and `reminderPlan` tests, `repeatRule.test.ts`, `seriesVisits.test.ts`, `todoRepeat.test.ts`.
- **Integration** (the fake Google client, the memory sender): a pull after deleting a past event's and a todo's Google event changes nothing in Caretook; a pull takes an event 200 days out and a later time change to it; the catch-up takes an event that came inside the horizon, once, and skips linked events; a push leaves out a soft-deleted provider (and the feed does); the consent rule across the settings view, feed, push and reminder; an expired reminder is skipped and a retry is never scheduled past expiry. Prior art: `calendars.int.test.ts`, `reminders.int.test.ts`, `outbox.int.test.ts`.
- **Component:** the Details card when the consent is out of date and after agreeing again (`settings/calendarReminders`); the Repeat sheet's monthly choices on the 31st and on a mid-month day, for an event and a todo (`plannerSeries`, the todo repeat tests).
- **No new end-to-end steps:** nothing here changes a sync stream or a flow across devices. The Google smoke test stays manual; the fake's `listWindow` mirrors what the real one asks Google for (live events, bounded), which the smoke test can confirm.

## Out of scope

- Push notifications (B-377): topic `calendars-3`.
- Turning a Google repeat into a Caretook repeat, and taking titles, places or descriptions edited in Google.
- A member's own time zone or language for reminders, and the en-GB dates on the settings cards: the translations topic (`i18n`).
- Re-rendering a queued reminder when its event moves; alerting when the calendar cron fails (B-351, hosting).
- Telling a member by email or in the app shell that their consent is out of date.
- Cleaning Google events pushed long ago for notes (they leave with the window).
- Revoking Google access on account deletion (no deletion flow yet).
